# Money Bridge: reusable approval for an exact financial action

Research date: **2026-10-17**. Audience: the Money Bridge owner and implementation agent. This is a research report, not a change to the binding decisions.

Required inputs read first: [DECISIONS.md](../design/DECISIONS.md) and [provider-routes.md, section 9](provider-routes.md#9-passkey-approval-of-the-exact-action). Money Bridge is MIT-licensed, independently installed by each owner, and uses official financial APIs. Every money movement requires a fresh owner passkey confirmation. The approval system must be reused.

**Source dates:** every linked external source below was accessed on **2026-10-17**; publication/release dates are supplied where established. Prices are the public USD prices observed on that date, excluding taxes, infrastructure, messaging, and negotiated terms. Undated documentation is a current documentation snapshot, not proof that a feature has always existed. No signups, credentials, enrollment, payment requests, or live authentication tests were performed. **Unverified** means the necessary behavior, entitlement, availability, or assurance was not established by this desk research. Estimates are explicitly identified.

## 1. Decision

**Do not choose a generic login/MFA product and call its success response transaction approval. No researched option is verified to satisfy all Money Bridge constraints together without an exception or further qualification.** In particular, passkey login, push approval, action binding, and enforcement of the resulting money movement are four separate capabilities.

Recommended course:

1. **Reuse provider-native approval wherever it covers the actual movement. Start with Link's spend-request workflow**, subject to confirming its fresh-passkey policy and the precise provider-enforced payment constraints. Never treat one Link approval as authorization for another provider's action. Do not enable unattended spending policies under the current decisions.
2. **For a reusable general approval service, qualify a bring-your-own Cosignet integration first.** Its documented protocol most directly matches exact-payload, fresh-passkey approval without writing our own ceremony or phone app. Each installer can have their own account; Money Bridge need not operate a central service. However, it is early access and stores the financial action payload in its cloud. **It is not an approved production default under the existing local-only data decision.** Qualification must resolve the items in section 8, and cloud disclosure would require an explicit product decision.
3. **Fallback under the decisions as written: keep money-moving operations disabled/read-only when no qualifying reused approval exists.** This is a definite product behavior, not a suggestion to implement WebAuthn ourselves. Auth0 CIBA + RAR is the strongest conventional hosted alternative to investigate if commercial identity infrastructure is acceptable, but mandatory fresh passkey approval is not established for the stock Guardian path. privacyIDEA is the strongest existing self-hosted push/biometric alternative, but its push signature is not a WebAuthn passkey and its stock push text is not a generic transaction-signing API.

If “fallback” means an immediately deployable universal service that meets every current decision, **none was verified**. Choosing a library, building a phone approval UI, substituting a biometric-unlocked non-WebAuthn device key for a passkey, or sending action details to a cloud provider would each change or interpret a binding requirement. This report does not make those changes.

The closest options, and the decisive remaining gaps, are:

| Option | What already exists | Decisive gap for Money Bridge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Link native approval** | Spend request, phone notification, review, provider-controlled release of payment capability | Link purchases only; fresh passkey on every request and complete exact-action enforcement need confirmation |
| **Cosignet** | Browser approval, required WebAuthn UV, payload-bound assertion, notification integrations, outbound polling | Early access; hosted payload storage; independent verification/enrollment integration needs qualification |
| **Auth0 CIBA + RAR + Guardian** | Real async authorization, existing iOS/Android app, structured details | Paid entitlement; stock Guardian's mandatory per-action passkey assurance unverified |

| **ClawGate public server** | Approval API, WebAuthn registration/authentication, Web Push plumbing | **Do not adopt:** source-level approval verification gap, described in section 5 |
| **WebAuthn libraries + push** | Maintained cryptographic ceremony implementations and notification transports | Still requires us to build the approval application, contrary to the current decision |

Evidence and qualifications for every entry follow. “Closest” describes documented semantics, not an independent security certification.

## 2. What counts as approval

The required outcome is approval of **one immutable intent**, not a reusable authenticated session. At minimum it must identify owner, provider, operation, exact source, exact destination, amount and currency, expiry, and a unique intent identifier. Represent monetary amounts in integer minor units with an explicit currency; define decimal precision and canonical serialization for quantities instead of hashing floating-point display text. For trades, also include the instrument, buy/sell, quantity or notional, order type, price constraints, time in force, and permitted execution conditions. A market order cannot promise an exact final price: if the requirement is an exact amount, use appropriate price/fee constraints or reject unsupported execution semantics. These are **design consequences**, not capabilities promised by an identity vendor.

Three assurance levels should remain distinct:

- **Identity/step-up:** “This user recently authenticated.” It is insufficient by itself.
- **Authorization-server approval:** an authenticated server result is correlated to a unique pending request and frozen action. This can enforce a local policy when the authorization server and local mapping are trusted. It need not contain an independently verifiable user signature over the action.
- **Portable action evidence:** the owner's enrolled credential signs a challenge bound to the complete intent; the executor can verify the assertion, identity, freshness and binding. This is the most useful fit for Money Bridge, but still depends on trustworthy display and execution.

**CIBA is a flow, not an authenticator app.** Its `poll`, `ping`, and `push` modes describe token delivery to the client; CIBA “push mode” does not mean that a phone push service is included. `binding_message` is a human-readable correlation message, not a standard payment schema or a guaranteed user signature over transaction data. Client-signed request objects protect the request from the client to the identity provider; they do not, by themselves, prove owner approval. [OpenID CIBA Core 1.0, 2021-09-01](https://openid.net/specs/openid-client-initiated-backchannel-authentication-core-1_0.html).

**RAR adds structured authorization details.** RFC 9396 provides `authorization_details` and transaction-oriented examples, but each authorization server must implement the relevant type, display, grant, and token semantics. A broadly scoped OAuth token is not automatically single-use or restricted to the amount/payee shown in a message. [RFC 9396, May 2023](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396.html).

**WebAuthn UV is not a Face-ID-only guarantee.** A passkey authenticator signs authenticator data and the hash of client data containing the challenge and origin. A challenge can bind a nonce and action digest, but ordinary authenticator UI does not reliably display the payment details. UV can be satisfied by an allowed PIN/passcode, and a synced passkey need not stay on one phone. The deployment must distinguish “owner passkey verified” from “a particular biometric was used.” [WebAuthn Level 3 Recommendation, 2026-08-25](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/REC-webauthn-3-20260825/).

**Neither a good signature nor an identity provider executes the frozen bank request for us.** The final submit process must hold the financial credentials, atomically consume approval, reject parameter substitutions and replay, and reconcile ambiguous provider responses. If an agent has the raw trading/payment token or can rewrite the executor as the same privileged OS user, an advisory approval hook can be bypassed. This is an architectural requirement shared by every option below.

### Auth0 deserves a qualification spike, not an unconditional selection

Auth0 already supplies the most awkward conventional pieces: asynchronous request lifecycle, user addressing, an existing phone app, and a renderer for RAR fields. Its stock Guardian schema can display Money Bridge fields such as amount, source, destination, operation, and expiry without writing a custom native app. Put the complete operation and intent identifier in the structured data; a short binding message alone is insufficient. The official .NET SDK example also exposes returned authorization details, but permits them to be absent: the executor must reject missing/mismatched authorization rather than infer success. [RAR schema](https://auth0.com/docs/get-started/apis/configure-rich-authorization-requests), [official .NET RAR/CIBA example](https://github.com/auth0/auth0.net/blob/master/Examples.md).

The blocker is assurance, not the availability of a consent button. General support for passkey login does not establish that every Guardian CIBA approval uses a fresh owner passkey. **Unverified:** a supported configuration of the stock app that makes every action fail unless that exact fresh passkey ceremony occurred, with usable evidence returned to Money Bridge. Do not resolve this by quietly adding our own phone ceremony. Also disable fallback channels that would lower the assurance, and do not use refresh tokens or remembered consent as authorization for a later movement. These are proposed integration conditions, not verified defaults.

### privacyIDEA: two particularly easy mistakes to avoid

The `app_force_unlock` policy in the v3.14 documentation can require biometric unlock when accepting or declining a push. This is stronger than a voluntary app lock, but the documentation explicitly says it does not establish biometric-bound storage of the signing key. Its push protocol signs the server's notification fields; the phone's acceptance signature covers the nonce and token serial, with additional response fields where applicable. The action therefore depends on a trusted immutable server mapping, rather than being a portable owner passkey signature over an action digest. [v3.14 enrollment policy](https://github.com/privacyidea/privacyidea/blob/v3.14/doc/policies/enrollment.rst), [v3.14 push implementation](https://github.com/privacyidea/privacyidea/blob/v3.14/privacyidea/lib/tokens/pushtoken.py).

More importantly, do not put `{challenge}` into `push_text_on_mobile` and assume a payment appears. The v3.14 authentication-policy documentation says that tag is filled for transaction-data token types such as OCRA, while tags not filled for push become empty. A safe dynamic per-action display API, including behavior during phone polling, must be established before selecting it. Rewriting policies globally for each pending payment is not a demonstrated safe integration. [v3.14 authentication policies](https://github.com/privacyidea/privacyidea/blob/v3.14/doc/policies/authentication.rst).

The existing app can poll without Firebase, but poll-only is not “the closed phone app immediately receives a prompt.” The maintainer's **2022** app-4.0 announcement explains that stock app push needs NetKnights' central project subscription or an app recompile; current server docs still describe the managed Firebase project. **Unverified:** today's individual subscription price and whether a newer supported delivery option removes that dependency for the stock iOS app. [Maintainer announcement](https://community.privacyidea.org/t/privacyidea-authenticator-4-0-with-push-poll/2515), [official v3.11 manual](https://privacyidea.readthedocs.io/_/downloads/en/v3.11/pdf/).

### ClawGate: concrete source-level rejection

Static inspection of the public `main` snapshot found that the approval handler parses `passkeyResponse` but does not verify it, then marks the pending request approved. That route has no authentication middleware, and the inspected Express mount does not add passkey verification. Separate passkey authentication endpoints do verify assertions, but the approval path does not enforce their completion. In addition, issued tokens contain action/scopes/request IDs rather than a complete action digest, and the token-verification route does not atomically consume a token. **Do not use this snapshot as Money Bridge's approval authority.** This is a source-level finding, not a live exploit test, exhaustive audit, or proof about the hosted deployment. [Approval routes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AsteyaTech-com/clawgate-api/main/src/routes/approval.ts), [server mount](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AsteyaTech-com/clawgate-api/main/src/index.ts), [separate passkey routes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AsteyaTech-com/clawgate-api/main/src/routes/passkey.ts).

### Cosignet: closest small-service fit, with explicit limits

Use its **REST API from the trusted executor**, rather than trust an agent's statement that its MCP tool returned approved. Long-polling lets each installation operate behind NAT without an inbound webhook. Include the operation name, owner/install identifier, financial fields, nonce/intent ID and approval expiry **inside the signed payload**. Do not assume that a top-level action label or API TTL outside that payload is owner-signed. Use `public: false`; financial payload disclosure into a public transparency bundle is unnecessary. These are **proposed integration rules**. [REST/MCP documentation](https://cosignet.com/docs).

The vendor documents a public verifier for the WebAuthn signature and log proof, and publishes a TypeScript/Python SDK/verifier repository. That is useful evidence, but not an independent audit. A local verifier must pin the owner's credential/public key through trusted enrollment and verify origin/RP ID/UV/challenge, rather than trust the API's status or any arbitrary key returned with it. **Unverified:** complete private-account credential export/enrollment contract, canonicalization compatibility, fresh-phone behavior, and safe key recovery/replacement. [Verification page](https://cosignet.com/verify), [SDK repository](https://gitlab.com/cosignet/sdk).

The provider's web page remains part of the trusted display boundary. Standard WebAuthn UI confirms the relying party, not the amount/payee. A page re-hashing its own displayed payload or adding a transparency-log entry does not by itself protect against compromise of that page. This is a design limitation shared with other ordinary WebAuthn approval pages; SPC addresses a different trusted-display model.

There is also documentation drift: the main docs describe notification messages as link-only, whereas the security page says a linked Telegram notification includes the action label. Treat notification metadata exposure as **unresolved**, and keep sensitive data out of labels. More fundamentally, Cosignet explicitly stores the full action payload and raw assertion. Sending only an opaque local ID would preserve less data, but then the stock page could not show the exact amount/from/to. It does not solve local-only data storage. [Data handling/security](https://cosignet.com/security).

## 8. Qualification contract for a reused service

These are concrete unanswered acceptance questions for the implementation team. They were **not executed** in this research task and do not authorize signups or credentials.

1. **Enroll the correct owner without agent control.** Establish a trusted credential/public-key record; forbid an agent API key from creating/replacing the approving owner or weakening assurance. Verify recovery and phone replacement rules.
2. **Show and bind the complete financial intent.** Amount/currency, exact source/destination, normalized operation, maximum fee, expiry, intent/install/provider identity and constraints must all be displayed from the immutable object. Friendly payee names alone are insufficient. Every signed field must have a defined canonical representation.
3. **Prove fresh per-action passkey verification.** Existing sessions, a push tap, prior MFA, refresh tokens, generic `acr/amr`, or a recent-login window must not substitute. Reject timeout, denial, missing evidence and weaker factor/channel fallback.
4. **Verify evidence locally where available.** Check trusted credential, signature, challenge, RP ID/origin, UV, intended action digest, subject and expiry. For authorization-server evidence, validate issuer/audience/signature and the trusted request association. Hash equality alone is not complete WebAuthn verification.
5. **Execute once.** Bind evidence to one frozen intent and atomically consume it before final submission. Require new approval for any changed amount, source, payee, fee cap, order terms or date. Use provider idempotency and reconcile unknown submission outcomes.
6. **Meet the phone and independent-install promise.** Existing iPhone/Android app or browser flow; stable documented origin; no Money Bridge central service; documented account/production entitlement and cost per independent owner. Phone-only enforcement, as distinct from any allowed synced passkey, must be defined if required.
7. **Resolve storage/privacy before selection.** Document payload, public-key, assertion, log and notification storage, retention/export and subprocessors. Neither “only an opaque ID in the cloud” nor “only a digest displayed on the phone” delivers the requested stock exact-action UI. Cloud action disclosure needs an explicit exception to the existing local-data rule.

For Cosignet specifically, establish private credential export/pinning, canonical payload rules, signed expiry semantics, immutable status behavior, recovery, service failure behavior and any notification-label disclosure. Verify published free-tier limits rather than assume a production SLA. Its security page currently says automatic deletion is not yet implemented, while pricing advertises audit-history windows; **history visibility must not be treated as a deletion guarantee**. [Security/retention](https://cosignet.com/security), [pricing](https://cosignet.com/#pricing).

For Auth0 specifically, establish the paid SKU/limits, stock Guardian rich display, returned authorization details, and supported policy/evidence for a fresh passkey on every CIBA action. For privacyIDEA, establish dynamic transaction display through supported APIs and decide whether a biometric-unlocked challenge key is even acceptable instead of a passkey. Until resolved, neither is a verified replacement for the exact requirement.

## 9. Final recommendation and glue boundary

**Recommendation:** retain a provider-independent approval interface, implement provider-native approval first, and select no generic production signer on the strength of login/MFA marketing. **Cosignet is the first general service to qualify**, because it already documents exact-payload WebAuthn approval, an owner-facing page, notifications and verifiable evidence without a custom phone app. Its cloud storage and early-access assurance are explicit adoption blockers under today's decisions, not implementation details to hide.

**Fallback:** fail closed/read-only for unsupported money-moving actions. If the owner later permits a commercial cloud approval exception, compare Cosignet with Auth0 CIBA/RAR using section 8; Auth0 has a stronger conventional identity platform but its stock per-action passkey guarantee remains unverified. If full self-hosting is paramount and a biometric device-key signature is accepted as a policy change, evaluate privacyIDEA; do not pretend it already provides generic passkey transaction signing. **Do not fork ClawGate or implement a SimpleWebAuthn approval UI as an implicit fallback.**

**Glue Money Bridge still owns, whichever reused product wins:** a versioned immutable financial intent; the backend adapter; trusted owner/credential configuration; evidence validation; approval expiry/replay handling; an isolated credential-holding executor; provider idempotency and reconciliation; and local audit records. The agent receives a pending intent/result, not financial credentials or authority to bypass approval. Reused providers own enrollment ceremonies, authenticator integrations, the approval surface and delivery mechanisms to the extent their documented product actually supplies them.

This gives the implementation team an honest boundary: integrate a qualifying existing approval system, and expose an action only when its approval and execution contract is proven. Broader bank coverage does not justify weakening the owner's per-movement passkey decision.
